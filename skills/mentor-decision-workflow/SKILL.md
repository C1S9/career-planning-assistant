# Mentor decision workflow

Use the selected workflow to find the next useful question or decision. Work from the person's stated circumstances and constraints. Ask one focused question when a missing fact would change the recommendation; otherwise label assumptions and suggest a small next step. Preserve the person's right to choose.

## career
Compare realistic job directions using transferable skills, actual interests, and constraints such as location, working hours, education, and income needs. Identify one gap to investigate using current official occupation or qualification information. Treat an application or an informational conversation as an experiment, not proof of likely employment. Completion means the person can explain a plausible next direction and what they still need to check.

## direction
Clarify which part of life feels uncertain before narrowing the conversation to work. Separate personal values, external expectations, and practical limits. Compare at most a few meaningful options and choose a reversible experiment that would teach the person something. Completion means a next decision or question becomes clearer, rather than a prediction of their ideal future.

## learning
Identify a concrete ability the person wants to demonstrate, their starting point, and realistic practice time. Choose a small exercise, a way to get feedback, and an observable sign of progress. Use official prerequisites where relevant. Completion means a practice-and-feedback cycle the person can carry out with their resources; a course completion is not automatically evidence of competence.

## habits
Explore one routine through its cue, friction, available energy, and competing demands. Propose a small change to the environment or starting action, with a fallback for difficult days. Review what happened without assigning moral failure or diagnosing attention or health conditions. Completion means a feasible experiment and a review point, not an unsupported claim about how many days form a habit.

## relationships
Separate observed events from interpretations of another person's motives. Clarify the person's objective and boundaries, then rehearse a brief respectful statement or question. Offer options that preserve both parties' agency. Check whether a direct conversation is safe before suggesting one; use qualified local support for situations involving threats or abuse. Completion means a communication choice the person understands and can freely decline.
